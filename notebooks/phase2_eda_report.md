## Known data-generation quirk (see data/SCHEMA.md)

The raw `day_name` column does not reliably match the weekday computed from `date` (14/100 match in the first 100 rows); for the remaining rows it was replaced with a generic `weekday`/`weekend` bucket and the real weekday leaked into a stray 17th CSV column caused by a trailing comma in the header. Both raw columns are dropped at ingestion; `day_of_week`/`is_weekend` here are recomputed from `date` instead.

## Notable directional patterns (descriptive, not causal)

- More `calories_burned`, higher `hunger_level`, and longer `workout_duration_min` all associate with a higher `roti_count` — the two strongest being calories burned (r=0.58) and hunger (0.53).
- Rest days (`workout_type='none'`) average the fewest rotis (2.0); cardio days the most (3.0).
- `curry_richness='heavy'` days average notably more rotis than `light` (3.15 vs 2.20).
- `dal` days average fewer rotis than `dry`/`gravy` days (2.06 vs ~2.7-3.1).
- `weight_trend` and weekend/weekday show little difference in `roti_count` on their own.

## Implication for Phase 3

- No imputation strategy is needed for this dataset version — the flagging scaffolding exists but is currently inert. Revisit if/when a data refresh introduces real gaps.
- `calories_burned`, `hunger_level`, `workout_duration_min`, and `curry_richness` look like the most informative features for `roti_count` from this first pass; final feature selection is a Phase 3 decision, not concluded here.
- 220 days is enough for a chronological hold-out but is a small dataset — favor simple, regularized tabular models over anything with many parameters.
